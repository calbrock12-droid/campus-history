Bandelier Hall East, located at 401 Terrace Street NE on the University of New Mexico's main campus in Albuquerque, represents nearly a century of architectural and academic development. Constructed in 1930, the building has served multiple purposes, including a dining hall, bookstore, registration center, and academic facility. Its history reflects the university's changing needs and its commitment to preserving the architectural character of New Mexico.

The building was designed in 1930 by architect Miles Brittelle in the Spanish-Pueblo Revival style. This architectural movement combined elements of Spanish colonial and Pueblo building traditions, creating a distinctive appearance associated with the American Southwest. Bandelier Hall incorporated a Mission-inspired parapet, a decorative wall niche known as a nicho, and a central entrance hallway called a zaguan. These features contributed to the university's regional architectural identity. According to a 2018 UNM Newsroom article, the building's original façade remained largely unchanged for 88 years, except for the addition of a portico in 1941. 

Bandelier Hall was named in honor of Adolph Francis Alphonse Bandelier, a Swiss-born anthropologist known for his research into Native American cultures and Southwestern history. Bandelier approached anthropology through multiple disciplines, studying the relationships between history, geography, archaeology, and Indigenous societies. Naming the building after him connected the university's physical campus to the broader history of scholarly research in the Southwest. However, the available university accounts do not identify the individuals responsible for selecting the name or explain the circumstances surrounding that decision.

When Bandelier Hall first opened in 1930, it operated as a dining hall, providing a gathering place for members of the university community. As UNM expanded, the building underwent several changes in function. Over subsequent decades, it housed a bookstore, administrative offices, language laboratories, and a registration center. Eventually, it became associated with the Department of Geography and Environmental Studies, accommodating classrooms, laboratories, and faculty offices. The exact dates of several of these transitions are not established in the university's published historical summary. 

One major development occurred in 2018, when Bandelier Hall East underwent extensive renovations. Beginning in May, construction crews removed aging ceiling materials and began replacing outdated heating, ventilation, and air-conditioning systems. The project also introduced energy-efficient LED lighting, improved plumbing, and renovated restrooms. On May 21, the department reported that asbestos treatment had begun in the basement. By July 19, the renovations were running behind schedule, although new air-conditioning systems were operating and ceiling installation was progressing. These improvements demonstrated the challenges of maintaining an aging academic building while accommodating modern educational requirements. 

Bandelier Hall also received public recognition in 2018 when the UNM Parent Association selected it for the university's annual collectible holiday ornament. Graphic designer Jana Fothergill created the ornament, highlighting the building's architectural significance. The decoration sold for $18.89, referencing the university's founding year, with proceeds benefiting the Parent Association Scholarship Fund. This recognition reflected Bandelier Hall's place in the university's architectural heritage. 

Further modernization efforts emerged during the 2020s. In October 2023, university officials requested approval for a project adding automatic fire protection and alarm systems. In May 2024, another proposal outlined approximately $679,000 in interior improvements, including asbestos removal, accessibility upgrades, and renovations to offices and restrooms. University planning documents classified Bandelier Hall East as a Tier 2 historical property, emphasizing the need to balance preservation with safety and functionality. These documents establish the proposed work but do not independently confirm that every improvement was completed. 

Today, Bandelier Hall East remains part of UNM's academic landscape, particularly through its association with Geography and Environmental Studies. Its transformation from a dining facility into a center for geographic education illustrates the changing priorities of the university. At the same time, its preserved exterior reflects the continuing importance of regional architectural traditions. Bandelier Hall East stands as an example of how historic campus buildings can adapt to new educational purposes while retaining visible connections to their past.
